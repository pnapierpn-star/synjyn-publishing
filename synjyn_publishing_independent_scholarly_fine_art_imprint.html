Imprint Manifesto & Archival Mission

Bridging Cultural Anthropology, Modernist Literary Critique, Western Esotericism, and Fine Oil Painting

Synjyn Publishing operates as an independent scholarly press and atelier dedicated to long-form interdisciplinary inquiry and enduring visual craft. Established by researcher, author, and fine artist Paul Napierskie—working under the artistic moniker SYNJYN—the imprint investigates how symbolic systems migrate across historical epochs, sacred architectures, modernist literature, and ecological consciousness.

Eschewing ephemeral digital trends in favor of archival permanence, the imprint curates a unified digital corpus spanning rigorous humanities monographs, structured research notebooks, and original oil paintings on canvas. Each publication and canvas plate is conceived as an artifact of contemplative study.

Section II • Semiotic & Anthropological Architecture

The Vegetative Matrix: The Rider-Waite-Smith Tarot, Anthropological Modernism, and Postmodern Semiotics

Published across SSRN (DOI: 10.2139/ssrn.7578619) and Academia.edu, The Vegetative Matrix examines the Rider-Waite-Smith Tarot as an organic semiotic system bridging Frazerian anthropological modernism, comparative ritual liminality, and postmodern sign theory.

Section V • Principal & Editorial Note

About Paul Napierskie & Synjyn Publishing

Paul Napierskie is an independent researcher, author, and fine artist whose work examines the structural continuity between symbolic ritual, literary modernism, and ecological consciousness. A member of Sigma Tau Delta, the International English Honor Society, his scholarly writing pairs close textual analysis with comparative cultural anthropology.

Painting under the studio moniker SYNJYN, he extends these same philosophical preoccupations onto linen and canvas—employing classical oil glazing techniques to render architectural thresholds, nocturnal landscapes, and contemplative emblems. Synjyn Publishing unifies both vocations under a single independent press.

Colophon

This digital edition of Synjyn Publishing (paulnapierskie.com) was composed in classic transitional and modern serif faces—principally Times New Roman and Georgia—in homage to university press monographs and nineteenth-century fine-art exhibition catalogs. Set upon a warm archival ivory ground (#FAF8F5) with deep charcoal ink (#1A1A1A) and antique burgundy rubrication (#6B2D2D), every plate, seal, and bibliographic record is engineered for clarity, permanence, and scholarly citation.
