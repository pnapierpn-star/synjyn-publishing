The Imprint

Synjyn Press (Synjyn Publishing) is the independent academic imprint of Paul Napierskie. It publishes interdisciplinary scholarship that sits between disciplines: historical anthropology, environmental sustainability, literary criticism, ritual and symbol, architecture, and material memory.

The press serves as the single publisher of record for Paul Napierskie's research archive, monographs, scholarly papers, theses, and visual editions, uniting all works under one name and one catalog.

Founder, Publisher & Author

Paul Napierskie (born September 1973 in Neosho, Missouri) is an American socio-cultural anthropologist, writer, educator, independent researcher, visual artist, and musician. He founded Synjyn Press and serves as its publisher, editor, and author.

He pursued higher education at Southern New Hampshire University, earning dual Bachelor of Arts degrees (B.A., B.A.) alongside graduate study in English Literature. His interdisciplinary scholarship bridges historical anthropology, environmental sustainability, literary criticism, semiotics, folklore, and Western esotericism—examining how communities construct meaning, adapt to environmental shifts, and preserve cultural traditions through spatial thresholds and material memory.

In The Vegetative Matrix, he applies postmodern semiotic analysis to the Rider-Waite-Smith Tarot, tracing structural and iconographic continuity from Hans Holbein the Younger's 1526 Danse Macabre woodcuts to Pamela Colman Smith's illustrations. His eleven-chapter monograph Liminal Rhythms I–XI synthesizes childhood memory in Sarcoxie, Missouri with Durkheimian social facts, modernist literature, and ritual theory.

Scholarship & Research Themes

Napierskie's scholarly work focuses primarily on liminality, sensory ethnography, Western esotericism, and spatial theory. His major monographs and papers explore the intricate relationships between institutional anomie, architectural decay, temporal suspension, and ritual migration across both literary texts and contemporary digital culture.

Visual Art & Music

Alongside his academic research, Napierskie is an active visual artist and musician. He produces print-ready visual artwork, book cover illustrations, and detailed engravings embodying steampunk and art deco motifs. In his musical compositions, he designs intricate instrumental arrangements and percussion frameworks tailored for atmospheric and horror fiction narratives.

    Sources Cited & Theoretical Framework

    Key theoretical, anthropological, and primary literary sources cited across Paul Napierskie's monographs and essays.

    • Bachelard, Gaston (1994) [1958]. The Poetics of Space. Translated by Maria Jolas. Beacon Press. Cited in analyses of domestic interiority, windows, and intimate spatial memory.
    • Boas, Franz (1911). The Mind of Primitive Man. Macmillan. Used to establish cultural and historical perception of physical environments over geographical determinism.
    • Durkheim, Émile (1895; 1912). The Rules of Sociological Method and The Elementary Forms of Religious Life. Drawn upon for theories of material social facts and collective effervescence in ritual and live music.
    • Eliade, Mircea (1957). The Sacred and the Profane: The Nature of Religion. Referenced regarding sacred thresholds, cemeteries, and abandoned ecclesiastical spaces.
    • Frazer, Sir James George (1911; 1922). The Golden Bough: A Study in Magic and Religion. Macmillan. Foundational to Napierskie's analysis of dying-and-rising vegetation deities, the King of the Wood at Nemi, and ritual succession.
    • Holbein, Hans the Younger (1538) [1526 blocks]. Les Simulachres et Historiées Faces de la Mort (Danse Macabre). Lyon. Primary visual source analyzed in The Vegetative Matrix alongside early proofs at the British Museum and Kupferstichkabinett Basel.
    • Kafka, Franz (1925; 1926). The Trial and The Castle. Examined as modernist depictions of bureaucratic labyrinths and inaccessible architectural thresholds.
    • Mauss, Marcel (1934). "Techniques of the Body" (Les techniques du corps). Applied to embodied knowledge, muscle memory, and rhythmic coordination in musical performance.
    • Ovid. Metamorphoses, Book X (lines 298–502). Classical primary text for comparative mythic studies on Myrrha, Adonis, and botanical metamorphosis.
    • Turner, Victor (1969). The Ritual Process: Structure and Anti-Structure. Aldine. Core framework for liminal states ("betwixt and between") and egalitarian communitas.
    • van Gennep, Arnold (1909). The Rites of Passage. Structural model of separation, transition, and incorporation applied to spatial and life-course thresholds.
    • Weston, Jessie L. (1920). From Ritual to Romance. Cambridge University Press. Methodological basis for tracing the migration of fertility rituals into Grail romances and modernist literature such as T. S. Eliot's The Waste Land (1922).
    • Woolf, Virginia (1927). To the Lighthouse. Primary literary text analyzed in studies of the empty house ("Time Passes"), domestic thresholds, and material persistence.
